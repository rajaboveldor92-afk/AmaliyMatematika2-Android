APPLIED MATHEMATICS 2 — 1.2 IMPROPER INTEGRALS
Language: English

1. Introduction
For an ordinary definite integral the interval is finite and the integrand is bounded. If either condition fails, an improper integral arises.

2. Type I improper integral
With upper limit +∞:
∫_a^∞ f(x)dx = lim_{ξ→∞} ∫_a^ξ f(x)dx.
A finite limit means convergence; otherwise the integral diverges.

Interactive pause 1:
When does ∫_1^∞ dx/x^α converge?
Answer: α>1.

3. Example
∫_0^∞ dx/(1+x²)=π/2.
The screen shows arctan ξ approaching π/2 as ξ increases.

4. Type II improper integral
If f(x) is unbounded at b:
∫_a^b f(x)dx = lim_{ε→0+} ∫_a^{b-ε} f(x)dx.

Interactive pause 2:
When does ∫_0^1 dx/x^α converge?
Answer: α<1.

5. Comparison
The p-integral criteria on [1,∞) and (0,1] must be distinguished carefully.

6. Final mini-quiz
Classify sample integrals as Type I or Type II.
